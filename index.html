Do Not Buy From SeatGeek

Documenting a bait-and-switch on ADA-accessible tickets for a Zach Bryan concert at State Farm Stadium

Verified Incident — August 2026
August 24, 2026
ADA Tickets Changed to Non-ADA Without Consent
Forrest Falk purchased ADA-accessible tickets for Zach Bryan at State Farm Stadium (Phoenix, AZ) on September 5, 2026. SeatGeek unilaterally changed the seats to non-ADA seating.
August 24, 2026 (Same Day)
Support Promises Resolution — Offers ADA Seating
SeatGeek support (Jonathan) acknowledges the error, takes "personal ownership," and offers two options: Option 1: ADA-accessible seating 12 rows up from original location, or Option 2: Full refund.
August 28, 2026
Customer Accepts Option 1 (ADA Seating)
Forrest confirms: "Let's go with Option 1... as I would still love to go to the concert."
August 28, 2026 (Hours Later)
SeatGeek Reneges — Bait-and-Switch
Jonathan emails back: "unfortunately, due to current inventory constraints, we are not able to provide [ADA seating] at this time." Only options now: non-ADA seats in Section 204 Row 8 Seats 12-13, or refund.

Email Evidence (Excerpts)

August 24 — SeatGeek Support: "I completely understand that non-ADA seats do not meet your accessibility needs, and I'm committed to correcting this error without delay... I'm taking personal ownership of this matter and will work directly to ensure your tickets are restored to ADA-accessible seating right away."
August 27 — SeatGeek Support: "Option 1: We have accessible seating available higher up in the section (12 rows from the current location). These seats would maintain the accessibility standards you need for a comfortable experience."
August 28 — SeatGeek Support (after customer accepted Option 1): "I sincerely apologize for the inconvenience. I understand that I previously indicated ADA-accessible seating would be available, and unfortunately, due to current inventory constraints, we are not able to provide that option at this time."

Why This Matters

  • ADA Violations: Changing accessible seating to non-accessible without consent may violate the Americans with Disabilities Act.
  • Bait-and-Switch: SeatGeek explicitly confirmed ADA seating availability, got the customer to commit, then withdrew the offer.
  • Broken Trust: "Personal ownership" and "will not rest until resolved" were empty promises.
  • No Good Faith: The "inventory constraints" excuse appeared only after the customer accepted the offer.
